Vulnerability Management

Vulnerability Assessment Labs

Hands-on scanning with Nmap, Nessus, and OpenVAS. Compared scanner results, found an unpatched CVE, and wrote a report with CVSS scores and remediation steps.

Lab 1: Scanning my own system

I ran a full Nmap and Nessus scan of my own machine, reviewed the open ports, and went through the findings. I also read up on real-world vulnerabilities, including Log4Shell and SMB exploits, to see how these issues are used in attacks.

  • Open ports such as SMB and RPC can be real entry points if they are not secured.
  • Even “low severity” and informational findings show how much information a system gives away.
  • Vulnerabilities are not always obvious, and attackers only need one weak spot.

Lab 2: Two scanners on a Windows target

On a Hack The Box Academy target I ran Nessus (on Windows) and OpenVAS (on Ubuntu). Each tool found things the other missed, which is a good reminder that relying on a single scanner in a real engagement can leave gaps. I found an unpatched CVE that could have gone unnoticed without a careful read, then wrote a full report covering severity ratings, CVSS scores, and remediation recommendations.

Both labs were run only against my own machine or an authorized lab target.
